These tables help illustrate the concepts of outer joins. A sporting club has inherited some equipment and has independently voted upon the club sports that they will offer. The problem is to identify those chosen sports that do not have equipment, and the equipment that can be sold off, since the sport is not going to be offered.

The two tables used in the join examples

The examples below compare a sport table with an equipment table. A row matches when equipment.sport_id equals sport.sport_id. The sample data deliberately contains missing matches so the difference between INNER and OUTER joins is visible.

How the tables relate

The join condition connects equipment.sport_id to sport.sport_id.

sport table

All sports the club plans to offer.

equipment table

All inherited equipment, including rows with missing or unmatched sports.

Intentional gaps in the sample data

  • Archery and Whitewater Rafting have no matching equipment.
  • Broom and Stone use sport_id = 10, which does not exist in the sport table.
  • Ironing Board uses sport_id = 9, which also does not exist.
  • Unassigned training cones has a NULL sport_id.

Join queries

1. INNER JOIN

Shows only rows where a sport and an equipment item match on sport_id. In the Venn diagram, only the overlap is returned.

Highlighted area = rows returned by the join
SELECT
    s.sport_id,
    s.sport_name,
    e.equipment_id,
    e.equipment_name
FROM sport AS s
INNER JOIN equipment AS e
    ON e.sport_id = s.sport_id
ORDER BY s.sport_id, e.equipment_id;
Archery and Whitewater Rafting are excluded because they have no equipment. Orphaned equipment is excluded because it has no matching sport.

2. LEFT OUTER JOIN

Shows every sport. If a sport has no equipment, the equipment columns are NULL. The entire left circle is returned, including the overlap.

Highlighted area = left table plus matches
SELECT
    s.sport_id,
    s.sport_name,
    e.equipment_id,
    e.equipment_name
FROM sport AS s
LEFT OUTER JOIN equipment AS e
    ON e.sport_id = s.sport_id
ORDER BY s.sport_id, e.equipment_id;
Archery and Whitewater Rafting still appear, but their equipment columns are NULL.

3. RIGHT OUTER JOIN

Shows every equipment item. If an item has no matching sport, the sport columns are NULL. The entire right circle is returned, including the overlap.

Highlighted area = right table plus matches
SELECT
    s.sport_id,
    s.sport_name,
    e.equipment_id,
    e.equipment_name
FROM sport AS s
RIGHT OUTER JOIN equipment AS e
    ON e.sport_id = s.sport_id
ORDER BY e.equipment_id;
Broom, Ironing Board, Stone, and Unassigned training cones appear even though no sport row matches them.

4. FULL OUTER JOIN

Shows all sports and all equipment, including unmatched rows from either table. Both circles are returned.

Highlighted area = everything from both tables
SELECT
    s.sport_id,
    s.sport_name,
    e.equipment_id,
    e.equipment_name
FROM sport AS s
FULL OUTER JOIN equipment AS e
    ON e.sport_id = s.sport_id
ORDER BY
    s.sport_id NULLS LAST,
    e.equipment_id;
This keeps the unmatched sports from the left side and the unmatched equipment from the right side, along with every normal match.

Find only the gaps

5A. Sports without equipment

Start with every sport, then keep only rows where the join failed to find an equipment record. This is the left-only portion of the Venn diagram.

Highlighted area = left side excluding the overlap
SELECT
    s.sport_id,
    s.sport_name
FROM sport AS s
LEFT JOIN equipment AS e
    ON e.sport_id = s.sport_id
WHERE e.equipment_id IS NULL;
Result: Archery and Whitewater Rafting.

5B. Equipment without a matching sport

Start with every equipment item, then keep only rows where the join failed to find a sport. This is the right-only portion of the Venn diagram.

Highlighted area = right side excluding the overlap
SELECT
    e.equipment_id,
    e.equipment_name,
    e.sport_id
FROM equipment AS e
LEFT JOIN sport AS s
    ON s.sport_id = e.sport_id
WHERE s.sport_id IS NULL;
Result: Broom, Ironing Board, Stone, and Unassigned training cones.

Teaching note: In a production design, you would usually add a foreign key to equipment.sport_id. This sample intentionally allows orphaned values so that unmatched rows are easy to demonstrate with outer joins.
