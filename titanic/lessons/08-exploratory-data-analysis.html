Operation 1

Shape & Summary Statistics

Start by understanding how much data you have and what the numeric columns look like.

df.shape

df[
    ["Age", "Fare", "SibSp", "Parch"]
].describe()
QUIZ ALERT
describe() focuses on numeric columns by default. It summarizes count, mean, standard deviation, quartiles, minimum, and maximum — but it does not explain why the values vary.
Read more than the mean. Compare the mean, standard deviation, minimum, median, and maximum together. A very large spread or extreme maximum may point you toward skew or outliers.

Operation 3

Counts vs Proportions

Counts answer “how many?” Proportions answer “how dominant?”

Counts

df["Pclass"].value_counts()

Useful when you care about raw volume.

Proportions

(
    df["Pclass"]
      .value_counts(normalize=True)
      .round(2)
)

Useful when comparing datasets of different sizes.

QUIZ ALERT
normalize=True turns counts into shares of the total. Counts and proportions answer different questions.

Two-Way Segmentation with crosstab()

A crosstab asks how two categorical variables vary together.

Operation 5

Segment with groupby()

Counts tell you how many. groupby() lets you compare numeric outcomes across segments.

df.groupby("Pclass")["Fare"].agg(
    ["count", "mean", "sum"]
)
Group by class➡️ Count passengers➡️ Average fare➡️ Total fare

Compare survival by passenger class by calculating the mean of Survived for each class. Since Survived is 0/1, what does the mean represent?

Operation 6

A First Glance at Correlation

Correlation measures how strongly numeric variables move together in a linear way, from -1 to +1.

df[
    ["Age", "Fare", "SibSp", "Parch"]
].corr().round(2)

✅ Appropriate EDA reaction

“Interesting. Fare and class-related variables may be related. Let's investigate more.”

🚫 Overreach

“This correlation proves one variable caused the other.”

QUIZ ALERT
Correlation is not causation. A weak coefficient should not be over-read, especially in a small sample. EDA notices the number; later statistics helps judge how meaningful it is.

Quiz Transfer Challenge

Numbers Can Be Valid and Still Be Meaningless Together

EDA is also where you notice when a mathematically valid calculation is conceptually wrong.

PassengerFareCurrency
Alice50USD
Bob50GBP

🚫 Misleading

df["Fare"].sum()
# 100

Python can add the numbers, but 100 what?

✅ Better first step

df.groupby("Currency")["Fare"].sum()
QUIZ ALERT
Mixed currencies or units should not be silently summed. Group by currency first or convert to a common currency before cross-currency comparison.

Operation 7

Good Hypotheses vs Vague Observations

A hypothesis should be specific, measurable, and falsifiable.

🚫 Too vague

“Some passengers had it better than others.”

“First-class passengers seem lucky.”

✅ Testable

“First-class passengers had a higher survival rate than third-class passengers.”

“Female passengers had a higher survival rate than male passengers.”

QUIZ ALERT
“Some advisors seem busier” is an observation, not a strong hypothesis. A hypothesis must define a measurable comparison such as mean value, count, rate, or proportion.
